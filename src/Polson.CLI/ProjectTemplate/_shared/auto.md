## Autonomous mode — nobody will answer

**The director chose to leave this run to you.** They want to see what the workflow makes without being asked
anything, so no question you ask will be answered, and waiting for one is time taken from the work. Where this section
and the instructions above disagree, this section wins.

- **Where the instructions say to ask — Q1, Q2…, "ask the director", "confirm with the director" — do not ask.** Take
  the answer you would have proposed, the option you would have put first, and carry on at once.
- **Record each decision as the question it replaced**, so the director can review afterwards what they would have
  been asked before: `Stage.note('Q1 (decided, autonomous): <the question> → <what you chose>, because <why>')`,
  and in `direction.md` mark the choice *decided, not confirmed*.
- **A gap in the brief is not a reason to stop.** A brief with no subject: choose one that suits the workflow and say
  what you chose. A file the brief mentions that is not in `documents/`: make the best piece the rest allows, and
  say what was missing.
- **The standards do not change, only who decides.** Every check still runs, a check that fails is fixed rather than
  waved through, and a figure that cannot be verified still stays off the screen.

### `questions.md` — what the client would have been asked

A design process begins by interviewing the client and the other stakeholders (Lupton, *Graphic Design Thinking*,
pp. 6–8 and 26–27). This run skipped that conversation, so it delivers what the conversation would have covered, as a
document the client can answer. **Keep it as you go**: add a question when you decide it, not from memory at the end.
Finish it before the run ends. The studio shows it beside the brief.

```markdown
# Questions for the client — <project>

This piece was made without asking you anything. These are the questions the studio would have asked, what it
decided in their place, and what would change if you answer otherwise.

## The questions, most consequential first

### Q2 — <the question, as you would have put it to the client>
- **What it decides:** <the parts of the piece that rest on the answer>
- **Decided:** <what you chose> — **because** <the evidence or reason, with its kind: brief, Qloo, research, assumed>
- **If you answer otherwise:** <what would change in the piece, concretely>
- **Who should answer:** <the client; or the person who can: brand owner, legal, the market's lead, the data's owner>

## What the brief did not say
Gaps that were not a numbered question but that an interview would have closed: a product shot or logo that was not
supplied, a claim nobody stands behind yet, a figure without a source, whether a market is one the product can be
advertised in. For each, what the run did instead, and the risk that leaves.

## How to answer
Reply quoting the numbers ("Q2: …"). Every answer that differs from the decision above changes the parts named
under it, and nothing else.
```

- **Order them by consequence**: the decision whose other answer would change the most comes first. A question whose
  other answer would change nothing in the piece is not worth the client's time. Leave it out.
- **Say what was decided and why in the client's terms, not the workflow's.** "Which kind of ad, for Lagos" rather
  than "Q5 register". Tell the reader what the evidence was, never a result id alone.
- **Name who should answer.** Some questions are not the client's to settle alone: a claim is answered for by
  whoever can substantiate it; whether a product may be advertised in a market is legal's; whether a reference
  suits a city is best answered by someone who lives there. Lupton's interview is with the client *and the other
  stakeholders*.
- **The same numbering as the `Stage.note`s and `direction.md`**, so each answer can be traced to the choice it
  changes.
- **Nothing here is a finding about the work.** `findings.md` stays the studio's own account of how the run went.
  `questions.md` is addressed to the client.
